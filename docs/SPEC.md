# Music Idea Generator — v1 Spec

## Goal
A browser app that generates **8-bar musical ideas** (chords, strumming, drums, bass), plays them with **realistic sampled instruments**, lets you **lock what you like and reroll the rest**, and **exports MIDI** to take into a DAW. Hosted free on GitHub Pages. No server.

## Core loop
1. Pick a genre (or Random), weirdness level, and optionally a key and tempo. Anything not set stays on Auto.
2. Hit **Generate**.
3. The idea plays on a loop.
4. **Lock** the parts you like and **reroll** the others.
5. **Export MIDI** when it's worth keeping.

## Genres (v1)
| Genre | Tempo | Feel | Harmony | Drums | Chord rhythm |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rock | 100–150 | Straight 8ths | Power chords, I–IV–V, bVII, minor-key riffs | Backbeat, kick on 1 and 3, crash on bar starts | Driving 8th downstrokes, palm mutes |
| Pop | 90–128 | Straight or half-time | I–V–vi–IV family, sus and add9 chords | Four-on-the-floor or half-time | Syncopated strums, piano stabs |
| Funk | 95–115 | 16ths, light swing | Dom7/9, min7, one-chord vamps | Syncopated kick, ghost snares, busy hats | Muted 16th scratches, chord stabs |
| Grunge | 72–118 | Heavy, often half-time | Minor/phrygian, chromatic power-chord moves (bII, bV, bVI, bVII); drop D or Eb tuning | Kick locked to the riff, half-time, washy ride/open hat in choruses | Single-note riffs, chugs, big chords, clean arpeggios |
| Alt metal | 84–112 | Tight 8ths/16ths | Minor/phrygian, i–bVI–bIII–bVII family; drop D / drop C# / D standard | Kick doubles chugs and stabs, band stops together | Palm-muted chugs with stabs, quiet clean verse into big chorus |
| Hard rock | 92–138 | Straight 8ths | Mixolydian/major, I–bVII–IV, blues licks; standard or Eb tuning | Backbeat, kick on the chord stabs, ride in choruses | Picked chord-shape hooks, chord stabs answered by licks, pedal riffs |
| Heavy metal | 112–176 | Driving 8ths/16ths | Minor/dorian/phrygian, i–bVI–bVII | Kick partly doubles the gallop, ride in choruses | Gallops, pedal riffs, twin harmony in thirds |
| Random | Any | — | — | — | — |

**Random** either picks one genre at random or **mixes parts across genres** (e.g. funk drums + rock chords + pop bass). It sets weirdness to at least medium.

## Parts

### 1. Chords
- Key: any of the 12. Mode: major, minor, dorian, mixolydian; phrygian and lydian unlock at higher weirdness.
- Progression comes from genre templates plus a random walk.
- Weirdness adds borrowed chords, secondary dominants, chromatic moves, odd extensions and chord changes that don't fall on the bar line.
- Instruments: **piano, acoustic guitar, electric guitar** (clean / crunch / distortion).

### 2. Strumming / comping
- A pattern library per genre on a 16-step grid: down/up strokes, mutes, accents, rests.
- Weirdness mutates steps, displaces accents and adds odd groupings (e.g. 3-against-4).

### 3. Drums
- Sampled kit: kick, snare, hats, toms, crash, ride.
- Genre patterns, with fills on bars 4 and 8.
- Weirdness adds ghost notes, displaced snares and polyrhythmic hats.

### 4. Bass
- Follows chord roots. Patterns: roots, root–fifth, octaves, passing tones.
- Locks to the kick drum where it can.
- Weirdness adds chromatic approach notes, rhythmic displacement and occasional non-root notes.

## Weirdness slider (0–100)
- **0–30 Safe:** genre-typical, always musical.
- **30–70 Spicy:** a few surprises per idea.
- **70–100 Weird:** odd chords and rhythms, genre rules broken on purpose.

## Controls
- Genre, key, mode, tempo (each Auto or manual)
- Weirdness slider
- Per part: instrument, volume, mute/solo, **lock**, **reroll**
- Generate all, Play/Stop (loops)
- Export MIDI

## Fixed for v1
- 8-bar ideas, one section each. Full tracks with sections are built from an idea (see `docs/TRACKS.md`).

## Non-goals (v1)
- Not a DAW: no note editing or piano roll
- No melody, no vocals
- No AI models, server or accounts
- No odd time signatures

## Tech (proposed)
- **Vite + TypeScript**, static site
- **Tone.js**: playback, timing, sampler
- **tonal**: music theory (scales, chords)
- **@tonejs/midi**: MIDI export
- **Samples**: free, CC-licensed packs (piano, acoustic and electric guitar, bass, drums). Check each license before use.
- **Hosting**: GitHub Pages
- Git from day 1 in the local folder

## Decisions log
- 2026-10-08 (export crash fix): After an audio export, live playback could fail with a Web Audio error: some sounds (bass voices, sample fallbacks) are built lazily, and when that happened while the offline export was rendering they were built in the export's audio context instead of the live one. Each instrument now remembers its own audio context and builds anything new inside it (`src/audio/context.ts`). Verified: playback before and after an MP3 and stems export, no console errors.
- 2026-10-08 (sound review, part 3: variety, width, loudness): **Variety.** Chorus chords filled 54–62% of a track (intro, three choruses, interlude, outro against two 8-bar verses). Now: when the length budget allows, the first verse plays twice through (16 bars) with Guitar 2 joining the second time; the interlude plays in about 40% of automatic tracks (it was always on); strummed intros pick the short and band intros (verse chords) more than the 8-bar layered one; strummed first verses are soft about three times in four (was two in three); half the time the verse avoids the chorus's instrument. Chorus chords now fill 45–52%; in hard rock and metal the verse uses a different sound from the chorus about two times in three (was about one in four); tracks run about 15 s longer on average. **Width.** All rhythm guitars are double-tracked: driven ones panned ±0.9 (was ±0.75), clean electric and acoustic ±0.65 (they were a single take in the middle), each take a little quieter; cymbals and toms spread wider. The bridge of the test track went from almost mono (side 23 dB under mid) to 10 dB under; choruses about 1.4 dB wider. **Loudness.** MP3, WAV and the stems zip's mix are mastered: measured integrated loudness (ITU-R BS.1770, checked against reference tones within 0.05 dB) brought to −10.5 LUFS (at most +9 dB) by a 5 ms look-ahead limiter that also catches peaks between samples, ceiling −1.4 dBFS, three passes. The test track went from −14.1 to about −10.7 LUFS. Individual stems stay unmastered. MP3 moved from 192 to 320 kbps: at 192 the encoder pushed limited peaks up by 1.2 dB to full scale, at 320 by 0.6 dB. Live playback is unchanged, so exports are louder than playing in the app (by design).
- 2026-10-08 (sound review, part 2: groove): **Hi-hat accents were reversed.** Patterns already accented beats, but only by 2–3 dB, and the drum-bus compressors ducked every hi-hat that landed with the kick or snare, so off-beats came out about 5 dB louder than beats (a pumping, mechanical feel). Cymbals now skip the bus compressors (their own path, +11 dB to match the make-up gain the compressors used to give them; cymbal level unchanged), respond more to velocity, and get a metric accent at playback and in MIDI (`cymbalAccent`: beats ×1.35, 8th off-beats ×0.75, 16ths ×0.45). Measured in a full mix: beats now about 3 dB over off-beats (5–6 dB on the hats alone). **Strumming follows the hand:** a downstroke between two 8ths becomes an up-strum and an up-strum on a beat becomes a downstroke (`naturalStrokes`, after the band pass and track shaping); following the kick had left full downstrokes on fast off-beats. About a fifth of strums are now up-strums. **Random only mixes genres that groove together** below weirdness 60: rock with pop, pop with funk, and the strumming and bass must also fit the drums (no funk drums under rock strumming). Ideas made with the same seeds can sound different from before.
- 2026-10-08 (sound review, part 1: lead, mix space, wrong notes): An exported rock track was measured (no stems needed: the track was still loaded, so it was re-rendered part by part). Problems found: no clear melody, a muddy and harsh mix with a dull top, one random out-of-key chord in every chorus, lead notes grinding against the chords, a stiff funk-drum groove, too much repetition, low loudness, a narrow image. Fixed here: **the lead is the singer** — Guitar 2 melodies sit higher (lead D4–A5, octave melodies G3–A4 plus the octave), driven Guitar 2 tones use a lead voicing (more low cut, a vocal mid push, more sustain, smoother top), rock and hard rock leads use more gain than the rhythm guitar, Guitar 2 is panned near the centre and gets +3 dB (piano as Guitar 2 +4 dB more); in the test chorus the lead went from 3.5 dB under the rhythm guitar to about 1 dB over it. **Mix space** — driven rhythm guitars get a 130 Hz low cut, a cut where they turn harsh in a mix (2.7–2.8 kHz, −2 dB crunch, −4 dB distortion), less treble shelf and a lower top (crunch 8 kHz, distortion 6.5 kHz): the distorted guitar's harsh band is about 5 dB lower; cymbals get +5 dB above 7 kHz (mix top end about 4 dB brighter). **Notes** — the chord generator's "weird chord" no longer picks a fully random chord below weirdness 70: it picks a colour chord bands actually use (major keys: bVII, bVI, bIII, iv, II, III; minor keys: IV, bII, V, V7, bVI, bIII); held melody notes (an 8th or longer, or on the beat) never sit a semitone from a chord tone or a tritone from the root (4.4% of held notes did, now 1.1%). Ideas made with the same seeds can sound different from before. Still to do: groove (accented hi-hats, down-up strumming, no funk drums under rock guitars), variety (quieter verses, less chorus reuse), loudness and stereo width.
- 2026-10-08 (amp switching fix): Changing amp sound at a section (e.g. clean verse → distorted chorus) made a split-second burst. The one electric guitar was re-voiced on a timer: the drive curves changed at once but the volumes 0.1 s later, so the ringing chord went through full distortion at clean-amp volume (peaks +14 dB, about 90 ms before the downbeat), and the first new chord, already scheduled as a single take, was then panned left for its whole length (14 dB lopsided). Now each amp setting is its own guitar, live as well as in exports: nothing changes while notes sound, the old section's last chord rings out on its own amp, and the new section starts on the new one exactly on the beat (measured: no burst, left and right even). Idle amps were also found to be expensive (six idle distorted guitars cost about half a CPU core): the lopsided drive curve had an even number of points, so silence came out as a tiny constant offset that kept every amp processing, and Tone.Filter's always-running setting signals did the same. Drive curves now have an odd number of points, guitars and piano use plain biquad filters (same sound), and the stand-in synths are only made if samples fail. Six idle guitars now cost about 36× less, and audio export got about twice as fast.
- 2026-10-08 (instruments per section): **Each track section picks instruments that suit it** (`src/sounds.ts`), like a band arranging a song, instead of one sound for the whole track. Every block keeps one sound (all verses alike, all choruses alike), picked from genre palettes by energy: quiet sections on acoustic, clean electric or piano (riff genres: clean or acoustic, never piano); choruses and interludes on the genre's main sound; the bridge contrasts with the verse and chorus where the genre allows; rock and hard rock sometimes step up to distortion for the last chorus and outro; grunge verses often go clean or crunchy against distorted choruses. Guitar 2 follows too: the hook keeps one instrument across the track (clean in quiet sections), loud sections get a lead tone, quiet ones a clean guitar or piano that stands apart from the rhythm part. Each block's pick has its own seed, so "New verse" can also bring a new verse sound. Across 800 test tracks, rock uses about 2.9 instruments per track, pop 2.5, funk and the riff genres about 2. The section strip shows each section's instrument, section notes list it, and **Sound** in the section tools picks the rhythm instrument by hand for every section of that kind (saved in the share link). Choosing an instrument in Parts still fixes it for the whole track. Playback switches instruments on the section's downbeat and lets the last chord of the previous one ring; instruments later sections need start loading as soon as the track is set. Audio export renders the switches too. MIDI gives each part the instrument it plays most (a MIDI track has one sound).

- 2026-10-06 (M2): Pitched samples first came from tonejs-instruments, but they sounded synthetic. Replaced with real recordings that have velocity layers and round robins: Salamander piano (CC-BY 3.0), and Karoryfer guitars and bass (CC0). The electric guitar is recorded direct, with the amp and cabinet simulated in the app. All samples together are about 6 MB, but instruments load only when they're selected, so a session loads about 3 MB.
- 2026-10-06 (M2): Odd and mixed meters from the spike are kept, even though "Fixed for v1" says 4/4 only. Auto still picks 4/4 at low weirdness.
- 2026-10-06 (M2): Added Grunge and Alt metal, which are riff-based instead of strummed. The guitar part is explicit notes (a one-bar riff cell per 4-bar section, repeated, with a turnaround). Drums lock to the riff and the bass doubles it an octave down. Sections can go quiet to loud: clean arpeggios on the clean channel, then distorted riffs. These genres aren't mixed with others under Random, because their parts are written together.
- 2026-10-06 (M2): Master volume slider (remembered per device).
- 2026-10-06 (M2): Electric guitar amp rebuilt: two soft clipping stages with interstage filtering, a tone stack, and a real 4x12 cabinet impulse response (Dauntless IR pack, free to share, not for resale) through convolution. The filter cabinet stays as a fallback. A corrective EQ for each amp setting was tuned by measuring band energy against a typical guitar-recording balance. Added vibrato on held notes, small tuning spread, string-damping noises, double tracks that never reuse the same take, and longer electric samples (4.5 s). The acoustic got EQ for body and sparkle.
- 2026-10-06 (M2): Distorted guitar sustain: a compressor before the drive (like a sustainer pedal) plus more gain. A held power chord now stays within about 2 dB for 4 s, where before it lost 16 dB in 2 s.
- 2026-10-06 (M2): Added a second guitar part ("Guitar 2") with its own instrument, volume, mute and solo. The chords row is now called "Rhythm". For each 4-bar section Guitar 2 picks a role that suits the genre and the rhythm part: lead melody (call and response), harmony of the riff (3rd/4th/5th/octave), octave melody, clean arpeggios with a tempo-synced dotted-8th echo, volume swells, a funk single-note line, or sitting out. It's a single take (not double-tracked), panned slightly right, and has its own seed. Decoded samples are shared between parts to save memory.
- 2026-10-06 (M2): Drums were 15–20 dB quieter than the guitars, very peaky, and the snare and cymbals were buried under the kick. Added rock-mix EQ per drum, plus a drum bus with glue compression, parallel smash compression and soft saturation. Drums now sit level with the guitars (within about 2 dB).
- 2026-10-06 (M2): Bass switched to the brighter babyblue bass (f/ff layers), through a rock bass rig: compressor, clean low path blended with a distorted growl path, string clank EQ and a glue compressor. The amount of growl depends on genre (clean, warm, grit, heavy), and on-beat notes are plucked harder. Before, it was about 9 dB too quiet with almost nothing above 1.6 kHz; now it sits 1–4 dB under the guitars. Master bus gain was lowered so the limiter isn't overdriven.
- 2026-10-08 (audio export): **Export MP3, WAV or Stems** of the idea or the whole track (`src/export/audio.ts`). Rendered faster than real time with the same instruments, amps, mix and section levels as playback (offline audio contexts at the live sample rate), with a 5 s ring-out, a fade at the very end, and one safety gain to stay below -0.3 dBFS. MP3 is 192 kbps (LAME via @breezystack/lamejs, LGPL-3.0, loaded unmodified as its own file only when needed); WAV is 16-bit; Stems is a zip (fflate, MIT) of the mix plus one WAV per instrument, taken after each part's fader and before the shared reverb and master bus, all scaled by the same gain so they add back up. Speed: a track is split into pieces at section boundaries that render in parallel on separate cores; each piece starts two bars early so ringing notes, echoes and compressors carry over, and pieces are crossfaded (30 ms) just before each section's downbeat (no clicks measured at the joins). A 2-minute track takes about 30 s with the page in front, about a minute when the tab is in the background (browsers slow down hidden tabs). Engine changes this needed, which also help live playback: notes are plain Web Audio voices that clean themselves up when they really finish (Tone's buffer source signalled "ended" on its own clock, which in offline renders came before any audio was made); rhythm instruments are only built when used; a lead Guitar 2 no longer builds an unused double-track amp; the stand-in cabinet is disconnected once the real one loads; guitars count as loaded only once their cabinet is in; the room reverb's impulse response is generated once in code instead of rendered per mixer. Each export gets the same file name as the MIDI (`…-track-<seed>.mp3`, `…-stems.zip`).
- 2026-10-08 (M5: MIDI export): **Export MIDI** downloads the idea, or the whole track in track mode, as a standard MIDI file (`src/export/midi.ts`, @tonejs/midi, MIT). Four tracks: Rhythm guitar, Guitar 2, Bass and Drums (GM drum channel 10, GM drum notes), with General MIDI sounds matching the chosen instruments. Written the way playback plays it: the actual chord voicings strummed low to high (up-strokes reversed, palm mutes and scratches short), riff and lead notes with their lengths, swing, accents, and each section's energy as velocity plus its overall level as expression (CC11). Tempo, time signatures (including mixed meters), section markers ("Verse 1", "Chorus 2"…) and the key as text (including a last-chorus key change). Key signature events are left out because @tonejs/midi 2.0.28 writes them wrongly. File names like `hard-rock-fsharp-minor-122bpm-track-<seed>.mid`.
- 2026-10-08 (M4: lock, reroll, track editing): **Keep / reroll** row for Song (key, tempo, time, plan), Chords, Rhythm, Guitar 2, Drums and Bass. Locked parts keep their seed when you Generate; locking any part also keeps the song, or the part couldn't stay the same. ⟳ rerolls one part while it plays, carrying on from the same bar. Parts that play off others follow them (the bass follows the kick; in riff genres the drums and bass follow the riff, so the bass can't be rerolled alone there). **Tracks** have their own seeds (structure, verse, pre-chorus, bridge, hook), so the selected section can be rerolled alone ("New verse", "New bridge", "New hook"); the chorus is the idea, so it changes through the parts row. Structure options: Intro (short, layered, full band), Pre-chorus, Interlude, Key change (each Auto/On/Off). Sections can be moved, repeated, removed, and new ones added after the selected one; changing an option goes back to the automatic order. Generate in track mode keeps track mode. **Shareable link:** the page address always holds the idea, its options and the track (seeds, options and edited order), and Copy link copies it; opening it rebuilds the exact same idea and track.
- 2026-10-07 (musicality, phase 2: tracks): **Harmony plan.** Verse, pre-chorus and bridge chords now come from written plans (I–IV–I–V, vi–IV–I–V, i–bVI–i–v, IV–V…) instead of random seeds. Verses and pre-choruses end on V or bVII to lead into the chorus, a pre-chorus never starts on the chorus's first chord, and bridges end on V. Riff verses sit on the home chord and move in their 4th bar. **The hook comes back.** Strummed genres get a lead hook (the idea's own lead melody if it has one throughout) that plays in the intro, a new 4-bar Interlude after the first chorus, the last chorus and the outro. Riff genres bring back their most riff-like 4 bars as the interlude. **Arrangement.** Optional pre-choruses (energy 3) before each chorus. New 8-bar layered intro: the hook alone, then the drums enter with a fill and the bass joins (sometimes two bars later). New transitions into choruses: a snare build (8ths, then 16ths, getting louder) and band hits (everyone on the same accents). Rock, pop and sometimes hard rock move up a key for the last chorus and outro. Each section's notes list its chords as Roman numerals. Optional sections are dropped when a track would run past about 3.5 minutes.
- 2026-10-07 (cohesion): The parts didn't listen to each other: the kick and the strummed-genre bass re-rolled their choices every bar, so they never locked with a repeating riff or groove. Now decisions are made once per spot in the bar and reused (kick repeats with the riff in 99% of repeated bars, bass repeats with the drum groove 100%). A band pass (`src/parts/cohesion.ts`) runs after all parts are written: pushed chord changes take kick, bass and guitar with them and tie over the bar line (crash moves to the push); the strum hits with off-beat kicks; under drum fills guitar and bass either hold a chord or hit the fill's rhythm with the drums; Guitar 2 doesn't play over fills. Guitar 2 leads get busier where the riff rests and sparser where it's busy, and avoid semitone/tritone clashes with the riff (1.2% of notes left). The strum leans harder into kick and snare hits.
- 2026-10-07 (musicality, phase 1): Ideas could not land anywhere near songs like *Sweet Child O' Mine* or *The Trooper*, because notes were picked step by step (a random walk), with no motifs, idioms or guitar logic. Added a motif engine (`src/parts/motif.ts`): a short pitch shape built from real melodic shapes, moved onto each chord in scale steps (so it stays in key), and developed over four bars as a loop with a turnaround, a sentence (idea, idea moved, broken up, ending) or a period (question ending open, answer ending home). New riff styles built on it (`src/parts/figures.ts`): picked chord-shape hook with a moving top note, chord stabs answered by a pentatonic lick, pedal riff, gallop. New genres: Hard rock and Heavy metal. Guitar 2 leads now use the motif engine too, gallops and pedal riffs get twin harmony in thirds, and a new "rhythm chords" role plays under a picked hook. The bass roots in 8ths under picked hooks and doubles licks. Phase 2 (track harmony plan, the hook returning across the track, layered arrangement) is next.
- 2026-10-07 (M3): Tracks MVP built. **Make track** grows the current idea into Intro · Verse · Chorus · Verse · Chorus · Bridge (Breakdown for alt metal) · Chorus · Outro, about 1.5–2.5 minutes at typical tempos (longer with long bars at slow tempos). Strummed genres: the idea is the chorus, the verse and bridge get new chords in the same key (the bridge starts away from home), and drums, bass and strumming are reshaped by energy. Riff genres: a quiet-to-loud idea splits into verse (first half) and chorus (second half); otherwise the idea is the chorus and a new verse riff is written. Transitions: crash into each section, a fill or (sometimes) a band stop before choruses. The track ends on a held home chord. Auto rhythm guitar goes clean in quiet rock sections. Each section also gets an overall level after the limiter (energy 1: −5 dB, 2: −3.5, 3: −2, 4–5: 0), because the amp and bus compressors otherwise even out verses and choruses.
- 2026-10-07: Full tracks with sections become M3 (scope in `docs/TRACKS.md`). A track grows from an 8-bar idea that you like, reuses 2–3 blocks of music (verse, chorus, bridge) at different energy levels, and the structure is automatic at first. Track editing joins lock/reroll in M4; MIDI export moves to M5 so it exports whole tracks. Milestones renumbered to match.
- 2026-10-07 (M2): Optional tempo box (40–240 BPM, empty = Auto from the genre's range). Changing it while an idea is loaded changes the tempo live without rerolling anything; clearing it brings back the idea's own tempo. A fixed tempo doesn't change any other decision, so the same seeds give the same idea at any tempo.
- 2026-10-06 (M2): Each part has its own seed (song, chords, drums, strum, bass) so M3 can reroll one part at a time. Bass follows the kick, so rerolling drums also changes the bass rhythm.
