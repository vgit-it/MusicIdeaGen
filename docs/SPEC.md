# Music Idea Generator — v1 Spec

## Goal
A browser app that generates **8-bar musical ideas** (chords, strumming, drums, bass), plays them with **realistic sampled instruments**, lets you **lock what you like and reroll the rest**, and **exports MIDI** to take into a DAW. Hosted free on GitHub Pages. No server.

## Core loop
1. Pick a genre (or Random), weirdness level, and optionally a key and tempo. Anything not set stays on Auto.
2. Hit **Generate**.
3. The idea plays on a loop.
4. **Lock** the parts you like and **reroll** the others.
5. **Export MIDI** when it's worth keeping.

## Genres (v1)
| Genre | Tempo | Feel | Harmony | Drums | Chord rhythm |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rock | 100–150 | Straight 8ths | Power chords, I–IV–V, bVII, minor-key riffs | Backbeat, kick on 1 and 3, crash on bar starts | Driving 8th downstrokes, palm mutes |
| Pop | 90–128 | Straight or half-time | I–V–vi–IV family, sus and add9 chords | Four-on-the-floor or half-time | Syncopated strums, piano stabs |
| Funk | 95–115 | 16ths, light swing | Dom7/9, min7, one-chord vamps | Syncopated kick, ghost snares, busy hats | Muted 16th scratches, chord stabs |
| Grunge | 72–118 | Heavy, often half-time | Minor/phrygian, chromatic power-chord moves (bII, bV, bVI, bVII); drop D or Eb tuning | Kick locked to the riff, half-time, washy ride/open hat in choruses | Single-note riffs, chugs, big chords, clean arpeggios |
| Alt metal | 84–112 | Tight 8ths/16ths | Minor/phrygian, i–bVI–bIII–bVII family; drop D / drop C# / D standard | Kick doubles chugs and stabs, band stops together | Palm-muted chugs with stabs, quiet clean verse into big chorus |
| Hard rock | 92–138 | Straight 8ths | Mixolydian/major, I–bVII–IV, blues licks; standard or Eb tuning | Backbeat, kick on the chord stabs, ride in choruses | Picked chord-shape hooks, chord stabs answered by licks, pedal riffs |
| Heavy metal | 112–176 | Driving 8ths/16ths | Minor/dorian/phrygian, i–bVI–bVII | Kick partly doubles the gallop, ride in choruses | Gallops, pedal riffs, twin harmony in thirds |
| Random | Any | — | — | — | — |

**Random** either picks one genre at random or **mixes parts across genres** (e.g. funk drums + rock chords + pop bass). It sets weirdness to at least medium.

## Parts

### 1. Chords
- Key: any of the 12. Mode: major, minor, dorian, mixolydian; phrygian and lydian unlock at higher weirdness.
- Progression comes from genre templates plus a random walk.
- Weirdness adds borrowed chords, secondary dominants, chromatic moves, odd extensions and chord changes that don't fall on the bar line.
- Instruments: **piano, acoustic guitar, electric guitar** (clean / crunch / distortion).

### 2. Strumming / comping
- A pattern library per genre on a 16-step grid: down/up strokes, mutes, accents, rests.
- Weirdness mutates steps, displaces accents and adds odd groupings (e.g. 3-against-4).

### 3. Drums
- Sampled kit: kick, snare, hats, toms, crash, ride.
- Genre patterns, with fills on bars 4 and 8.
- Weirdness adds ghost notes, displaced snares and polyrhythmic hats.

### 4. Bass
- Follows chord roots. Patterns: roots, root–fifth, octaves, passing tones.
- Locks to the kick drum where it can.
- Weirdness adds chromatic approach notes, rhythmic displacement and occasional non-root notes.

## Weirdness slider (0–100)
- **0–30 Safe:** genre-typical, always musical.
- **30–70 Spicy:** a few surprises per idea.
- **70–100 Weird:** odd chords and rhythms, genre rules broken on purpose.

## Controls
- Genre, key, mode, tempo (each Auto or manual)
- Weirdness slider
- Per part: instrument, volume, mute/solo, **lock**, **reroll**
- Generate all, Play/Stop (loops)
- Export MIDI

## Fixed for v1
- 8-bar ideas, one section each. Full tracks with sections are built from an idea (see `docs/TRACKS.md`).

## Non-goals (v1)
- Not a DAW: no note editing or piano roll
- No melody, no vocals
- No AI models, server or accounts
- No odd time signatures

On, the idea's hook riff (its most riff-like half) plays under the verses at verse energy, the way a song's main riff carries it. If the chorus would play that same riff, it becomes new music: its own chords from a chorus plan that leaves home and comes back (V bVII I, IV V I, bVI bVII i…) under big chords (sometimes chugs), with its own seed (**New chorus** rerolls it; the verse can't be rerolled then, it's the idea). Ideas that split with the riff first already worked this way. The riff comes back after the first chorus as an interlude leading into the next verse. Chords: the verse's are the idea's (edit them there or from a verse bar), the new chorus is its own block. On Auto about half of riff tracks whose idea has a hook riff (picked, lick, gallop, pedal, single notes) do it, a quarter otherwise. Checked over 40 tracks per riff genre with it on: no errors, the verse never plays the same riff and chords as the chorus; on Auto track lengths match before (max 210 s on fixed seeds; a few slow odd-meter songs run long with it on or off, as before).
- 2026-10-10 (song shape, step 1 of 3: long intros and outros): Toward tracks shaped like real hard rock songs (a riff that carries the song, a new ending section, long builds). New intro **Long build** (16 bars): the guitar alone for 4, the drums come in with a fill (light: closed hats, no crashes), the bass joins at bar 9, Guitar 2 (the hook, or a riff genre's second guitar) and the full band at bar 13 after a fill. On Auto about 1 in 8 strummed and 1 in 5 riff tracks get it. New **Outro** option (Auto / Short / Long): a long outro plays 4 bars of chorus, then its last 2 bars twice as a tag, then ends; Auto about 40%. Long sections are the first to shorten when a track would run past 3.5 minutes (long outro, then long intro → layered → short). A long outro's chords aren't editable (its tag doesn't line up with the chorus bars). Checked over 40 tracks per genre: layers enter exactly at bars 5/9/13, no errors, track lengths still under 3.5 minutes on fixed seeds. Next: the riff through the verses, then an outro jam.
- 2026-10-10 (set your own chords: per section in tracks): In a track, tap a bar of a verse, pre-chorus or bridge (or breakdown) to set that block's chords: every verse (pre-chorus, bridge) plays them, and the block's own chords are written around them as on the idea (approach chords, power chords in heavy riffs). Chorus and outro bars edit the idea's chords (they're built from it). Intros and interludes can't be edited (they borrow other blocks); the hint says where you can. A section you edit loops so you hear it; repeats are marked too (a verse played twice shows your chord in both passes). The sheets live in the track options (saved in the link); Reset chords clears the shown block's. Rerolling a block keeps its chords and writes new ones around them. Riff ideas that split into verse and chorus halves: chords set on the verse keep the same riff, written to them. Sections whose chords you set keep their last bar (no turnaround) except into a key change. Checked over 40 tracks per genre (all 7) with random chords on every block: every chord lands where set in every section and in the editor, no errors; choruses only change in their last bar (leading into your new chords).
- 2026-10-10 (set your own chords: every genre): Riff genres (hard rock, heavy metal, grunge, alt metal) now take chords you set too. Their riffs, drums and bass are written from the chords, so your chords go in before the riff is written; a riff track's chorus (and, where an idea splits into verse and chorus halves, its verse) plays them. Where the riff plays power chords only, the editor offers the key's chords and the ideas as power chords (E5, A5…), and a free chord leading into yours is a power chord too; any other chord is still one tap away under Any chord. - 2026-10-10 (set your own chords): Tap a bar in the chord strip to set its chord, for the whole bar or each half (`src/parts/sheet.ts`, `src/ui/chordedit.ts`). Picks: the key's own chords (name and Roman numeral), **ideas that fit here** (a chord that leads into the next one, chords sharing notes with the one there now, the same chord with more colour, a borrowed chord; each says why), or any root and type. **Keep the chord before**, **Hold into next bar** and **Let it generate** handle chords held across bars. A bar left fully free keeps the generator's chords as written, pushes included (a push into one of your bars only if it's the same chord). Reroll Chords for new free chords only; Reset chords drops the sheet. Tracks: the choruses, interlude and outro (built from the idea) play your chords, and their turnarounds leave them alone (except into a key change); editing per section is next. Riff genres (hard rock, metal, grunge, alt metal) don't take set chords yet (the riff writes the chords there); the strip says so. Numerals follow the key's own scale (III in a minor key), flats against major for chords outside it. Checked over 337 ideas: every pinned chord lands where set; tracks still build.
- 2026-10-09 (setting up the next section): Feedback: some section changes still felt disjointed, with nothing at the end of a section setting up the next. Measured on 300–500 tracks: drum fills were almost everywhere, but (1) 8-bar layered intros never got any handover into the verse (only intros the guitar plays alone should skip it), and (2) big lifts (energy up by 2 or more, e.g. a quiet bridge into the last chorus) were often just a band stop or a push, with nothing building. Now layered intros hand over like other sections. Into a chorus after a big lift: a build is more likely (4), a push less (1), and a stop only when the section before is loud enough to stop (energy 3+). A big lift into a non-chorus: fill 3, build 2, push 1. Every big lift gets a **cymbal swell** (a reversed crash, up to 2 s, ending on the next downbeat; audio only, not in MIDI), the bass walks in more often (80% where it can), and the level rises over the whole last bar instead of the last beat. Riff genres and strummed genres alike. Also: crunch and distortion rhythm guitars sustain into the next strum with a short crossfade, more amp sustain and more of the shared reverb (+7 dB send); the bass stays close to clean in every style. A long (3.6 s) second reverb with a stereo chorus was tried for the driven guitars and removed the same day: it cost 11% more audio processing (measured on WAV renders of one fixed idea) and made phones crackle and then drop out.
- 2026-10-09 (thinking across bars, part 2: riffs and Guitar 2): Riff genres repeated one bar per four-bar run (single-note, chug and big-chord riffs). Now 55% of those runs are **two-bar riffs**: the second bar keeps the first half and answers it with new notes (3), a note or power chord held to the end of the bar (2), or, for big chords where the chord carries on, nothing at all, the first bar's chord ringing on across the bar line (the bass and drums follow). The answer comes back each time round; the turnaround at the end of the run stays. Motif-built styles (picked, lick, pedal, gallop) already develop over four bars. Clean **arpeggios** carry their pattern on across the bar line while the chord does, instead of restarting each bar. **Guitar 2** melodies (and hooks) anticipate: in half the melodies, notes that fall on a downbeat come an 8th early and are tied over the bar line (60% of chances), only where the note sits with both chords and doesn't clash with the riff. Measured over 1,200 ideas: the second riff bar differs in rhythm from the first in 82–86% of single-note, chug and big-chord riffs (was 47–60%); 20% of melodies tie a note over a bar line.
- 2026-10-09 (thinking across bars): Feedback: everything felt like it worked a bar at a time and reset at the next bar; a chord was struck in every bar, never held for two. Found: everyday strummed ideas always changed chord every bar (uneven chord rhythms only for weird ideas), the strumming, the piano and held bass struck again at every downbeat, quiet sections re-struck their held chord every bar, and the drum groove repeated bar for bar. Now: **chord rhythm** per idea: a chord a bar (4), each chord two bars (2), two bars then two quick ones (2), two quick ones then two bars (2), three bars then a change (1); 64% of strummed ideas hold a chord across a bar line, noted as "Chord rhythm: …". Where a chord carries on, the **strumming** can too (a player's habit, from the strum seed): restrike (2), tie over the bar line, the last strum ringing on (2), or leave the first half of the bar ringing and pick the pattern up halfway (1). The **piano**'s held chords ring on across the bar line (60% of ideas, at most two bars per strike); **held bass** roots aren't struck again (60%). Quiet sections (energy 1) strike a held chord on each change and again every second bar, not every bar; long bass roots likewise. **Drums** play the groove as two-bar pairs: the second bar of each pair turns around at its end (a kick pushing into the next bar, an open hat, or a ghost note; none in about a third of ideas), with the phrase-end answer and fills as before. Measured over 900 ideas: where a chord carries on, the guitar doesn't strike the downbeat 61% of the time; the second drum bar differs from the first in 65% of ideas.
- 2026-10-09 (layers come and go): Feedback: a layer (piano, strings, pad) played every bar of every section, doing something predictable; it should also shift the mood, be a contrast point, be held back or used sparingly. Now each added layer has a **role** for the song, from its own seed (`src/parts/layerplan.ts`): texture (part of the band, held back at first), mood colour (the quiet parts: intro, verses, a quiet bridge; gone in the loud choruses, maybe a few held moments in the last one), lift (saved for the big sections, arriving partway into the first chorus or the bars before it) or feature (held back for the bridge, the last chorus and the outro, with the odd hint before). Instruments lean to roles that suit them: piano colour 3, texture 2, feature 1, lift 1; strings feature 3, lift 2, colour 1, texture 1; pad texture 3, colour 2, lift 1; percussion lift 3, texture 2, feature 1. In each section the layer plays throughout, sits out, comes in halfway or for the last two bars, drops out halfway, or plays sparsely (piano and pad: one held chord at the start of each phrase; strings: a swell into the end of each phrase; percussion: the last bar of each phrase). A held chord already sounding when the layer comes in starts there (strings swell in). Once in, a layer stays in while the song builds (no dropping out at the bar line to come back partway); never silent for a whole song. A mood-colour layer can join an intro the guitar starts alone. Each section draws its own choice, so rerolling one block leaves the plan elsewhere alone. Ideas on their own: throughout, or coming in halfway (1 in 4). A new menu per layer, **Comes and goes** (default) / **Throughout**, keeps the old behaviour available; saved in shared links. The notes show each layer's role and what it does in the section. Measured over 280 tracks: layers play 47–70% of sections in full, sit out 21–43%, and come in, leave or play sparsely in the rest.

- 2026-10-09 (bass: held, flowing and driving lines; natural sustain): Feedback: the bass sounded like video-game background music, every line plucked and dying out; real songs also have continuous bass lines with intentional pauses. Found: playback cut every note at 1.2 s (2.5 s in riff genres), left a gap before each next note (92% length) and stopped with a 12 ms click-fade; the samples were trimmed to 2.5 s; and every line was short figures locked to the kick. Now strummed ideas pick a **bass line** (from the bass seed, so rerolling the bass can change it; shown in the notes): locked (the old kick-locked, plucked figures), **held** (long roots held into each other, sometimes struck again halfway), **flowing** (a connected line through chord tones, in one register, that walks into each new chord by a scale step or half step) or **driving** (legato root 8ths, walking into some changes). Held, flowing and driving lines leave a breath on the last beat of a four-bar phrase in 45–65% of ideas. Weights: rock locked 3, flowing 2, driving 2, held 1; pop locked 2, held 2, flowing 2, driving 1; funk locked 4, flowing 1. Tracks: quiet sections still play long held roots, loud ones driving 8ths (a flowing line keeps flowing). Playback: a note rings until the next one takes over (legato, small overlap), unless written shorter; up to 4.5 s. Notes stop with a 90 ms finger mute and sit 4–10 ms behind the beat. Bass samples re-encoded at 5 s from the local cache (bass folder 1.1 → 2.3 MB). MIDI export matches. Measured: held/flowing/driving lines sound 98–99% of the time (locked: 63%); a held note keeps its level for 3 s. Kick-and-bass together drops from 84% to 73% of kicks, since those lines don't follow the kick.
- 2026-10-09 (section handovers): Feedback: the connections between a song's sections still sounded abrupt. Measured on 350 tracks (2,826 section changes): most already had a drum fill, but everything else changed exactly on the bar line with nothing leading across it, and 3% of changes into a quieter section had nothing at all. Now each change can carry: (1) a **push**: the next section's first chord comes an 8th early with a crash and rings over the bar line, which isn't struck again (riff genres move the next riff's first hit); (2) a **ring-out** into a quieter section: one chord halfway through the last bar, left ringing; or a band stop for a breath; (3) a **turnaround** (strummed genres): when the last bar sat on the chord the next section starts on (70%), stepped to it by a half step or tritone, or goes into a key change, its second half moves to V (or IV, bVII) of the next section's first chord, the new key's V for a key change; bass moves with it, Guitar 2 notes that would rub move to a chord tone; (4) a **bass walk** into the next section's first note over the last beat (scale up or down, or chromatic from below; 55% of fills in strummed genres); (5) **Guitar 2 pickup notes**, one or two scale steps into its next phrase's first note, only lines that fit the chord under them (60% where Guitar 2 starts a section). Into a chorus: fill 3, build 2 (pre-chorus 4), hits 2, stop 2, push 2; into a section as loud: fill 3, push 1; into a quieter one: fill 3, ring 2, breath 1. Piano follows (pushed chord rings over, no downbeat restrike; ring-out chord held); the pad just follows the chords. Playback: note lengths look on into the next section (a pushed or ringing chord isn't cut at the bar line); the section level now glides down slowly (0.35 s) into a quieter section and swells halfway up over the last beat before a louder one. Result: per 2,826 changes, 14% pushes, 6% ring-outs, 12% bass walks, 14% Guitar 2 pickups, 4% turnarounds; pickups, walks and turnarounds checked against the chords (no rubs). Known, unchanged: in riff genres the bridge riff avoids the verse's style, so rerolling the verse can change the bridge (13% of tracks).

Rendered on its own and measured: the lead voicing cut everything under 400 Hz before the drive and pushed 850 Hz by 8 dB, under rhythm-level gain (sustainer +28 dB, drives 16 and 10): a held E4 came out with its fundamental 11 dB under the 2nd harmonic and the upper harmonics nearly flat to the 14th, a thin, nasal, square-wave buzz. Single-note lines no longer make a string-damping noise before every note (the drive blew it up) and change notes with a short crossfade. Riffs: twin harmonies follow only the riff's ringing single notes (harmonised palm-muted chugs were mush); the backing power chords under a picked hook hold or play on beats 1 and 3 (no more every-beat thumping); melodies stay close to the pentatonic (mode colour notes 10%+30%×weirdness each, was 25%+30%). The end-of-phrase hold/stop habit is now the band's (seeded from the song), so rerolling the strumming doesn't change the bass; strumming patterns get a per-take touch (as written, a breath before the next bar, or a choke on the last off-beat), so a reroll almost always changes the part (98%).
- 2026-10-08 (musicality round 1: phrases that breathe): Feedback: long unending streams of notes, then whole bars empty; no pauses, nothing held. Measured on 900 ideas and sections (default settings): Guitar 2 sounded 77–89% of the time when playing and only 11–22% of its four-bar phrases had even a two-beat rest (each note simply lasted until the next one); when it wasn't streaming it was usually gone for four whole bars. **Guitar 2 is a melodic guitar part, not a stand-in singer** (Paul): melodies, hooks and licks that leave room. A melody now picks a **shape** per four bars: *phrase and answer* (a phrase held into the next bar, a breath, then an answer in the same rhythm ending home), *long phrase, then space* (the motif and its sequence over two bars, held into the third, sometimes a pickup lick), *short repeated hook* (an instrumental hook in each bar with a gap after it, the last one ending home), or *short licks answering the band* (mostly silent, a lick at the end of bars 2 and 4). Every phrase ends on a held note followed by a breath; notes can be picked short and separate rather than always legato; phrase starts and strong beats are accented. Whole silent runs in ideas mostly became answering licks ("sits out" stays for riffs that are already licks, and in track arrangement drops). Arpeggios let the last note of each bar ring and stop a beat early at the end of the phrase; swells fade two beats early at the end of the phrase; the funk line is a one-bar figure with gaps (chosen from real funk figures), its notes taken from the chord under them, and the last bar of the run is cut short. Results: Guitar 2 sounds 43–63% of the time, 71–90% of phrases have a two-beat breath (melody shapes: all of them), held melody notes clashing with the chord stay at 1.3% (the clash test no longer counts the chord's own notes, e.g. the 7th of a maj7). Stabs use a funk figure with gaps per section; arpeggios leave the last beat to ring; the last bar of every four-bar phrase is one held chord (or a short figure and a gap). The piano plays about 40% fewer notes. Next: drums with character (round 2), rhythm guitar and bass with space (round 3).
- 2026-10-08 (piano and pad layers): Two **layers you can add and remove** in Parts ("+ Add piano", "+ Add pad", ✕ to remove), each with its own fader, mute and solo. **Piano** (`src/parts/layers.ts`): held chords, pulsing 8th-note chords, arpeggios or off-beat stabs, picked per section on Auto (by genre and energy: e.g. pop arpeggios in verses and pulsing chords in choruses, funk stabs, heavy genres big held chords up high above the guitars), or one style by hand. Quiet sections add a low left hand. All sections of a kind play alike; the player's habits (arpeggio direction) stay the same all song. **Pad** (`src/audio/pad.ts`): sustained chords that swell in and glide into each other, close or open voicing (one per song), the top note doubled an octave up in loud sections, a darker filter in quiet sections; tone Warm, Bright or Dark (Auto: warm for rock/pop/funk, dark for metal, grunge, alt metal). Both are written from the finished section, so they follow the arrangement: voice-led chord tones of whatever chord is sounding (power chords get the scale's third), the piano waits for the band in intros that start with the guitar alone, both stop for band stops, hit with band hits, and hold the final chord. Track sections now record how they end (`tail`) and where the band comes in (`bandFrom`) for this. With the piano layer on, Auto rhythm and Guitar 2 no longer pick piano (an acoustic or clean guitar plays instead), so two pianos never play over each other; the section's Sound note names what actually plays. Levels: the piano sits about level with the rhythm guitar in a chorus, the pad about 6 dB under. Each layer has its own seed (Keep / reroll shows Piano and Pad once added), the link saves which layers are on, MIDI gets Piano and Pad tracks (GM piano, Pad 2 warm), and stems add piano.wav and pad.wav (only for layers that are on). The pad's voices are plain Web Audio oscillators made per note: a first version on Tone's synth made audio exports 70% slower (each Tone voice carries several always-running signal nodes); now the pad adds about 16% and the piano about 30% to export time.
- 2026-10-08 (export crash fix): After an audio export, live playback could fail with a Web Audio error: some sounds (bass voices, sample fallbacks) are built lazily, and when that happened while the offline export was rendering they were built in the export's audio context instead of the live one. - 2026-10-08 (sound review, part 3: variety, width, loudness): **Variety.** Chorus chords filled 54–62% of a track (intro, three choruses, interlude, outro against two 8-bar verses). Now: when the length budget allows, the first verse plays twice through (16 bars) with Guitar 2 joining the second time; the interlude plays in about 40% of automatic tracks (it was always on); strummed intros pick the short and band intros (verse chords) more than the 8-bar layered one; strummed first verses are soft about three times in four (was two in three); half the time the verse avoids the chorus's instrument. Chorus chords now fill 45–52%; in hard rock and metal the verse uses a different sound from the chorus about two times in three (was about one in four); tracks run about 15 s longer on average. **Width.** All rhythm guitars are double-tracked: driven ones panned ±0.9 (was ±0.75), clean electric and acoustic ±0.65 (they were a single take in the middle), each take a little quieter; cymbals and toms spread wider. The bridge of the test track went from almost mono (side 23 dB under mid) to 10 dB under; choruses about 1.4 dB wider. **Loudness.** MP3, WAV and the stems zip's mix are mastered: measured integrated loudness (ITU-R BS.1770, checked against reference tones within 0.05 dB) brought to −10.5 LUFS (at most +9 dB) by a 5 ms look-ahead limiter that also catches peaks between samples, ceiling −1.4 dBFS, three passes. The test track went from −14.1 to about −10.7 LUFS. Individual stems stay unmastered. MP3 moved from 192 to 320 kbps: at 192 the encoder pushed limited peaks up by 1.2 dB to full scale, at 320 by 0.6 dB. Live playback is unchanged, so exports are louder than playing in the app (by design).
- 2026-10-08 (sound review, part 2: groove): **Hi-hat accents were reversed.** Patterns already accented beats, but only by 2–3 dB, and the drum-bus compressors ducked every hi-hat that landed with the kick or snare, so off-beats came out about 5 dB louder than beats (a pumping, mechanical feel). Cymbals now skip the bus compressors (their own path, +11 dB to match the make-up gain the compressors used to give them; cymbal level unchanged), respond more to velocity, and get a metric accent at playback and in MIDI (`cymbalAccent`: beats ×1.35, 8th off-beats ×0.75, 16ths ×0.45). Measured in a full mix: beats now about 3 dB over off-beats (5–6 dB on the hats alone). **Strumming follows the hand:** a downstroke between two 8ths becomes an up-strum and an up-strum on a beat becomes a downstroke (`naturalStrokes`, after the band pass and track shaping); following the kick had left full downstrokes on fast off-beats. About a fifth of strums are now up-strums. **Random only mixes genres that groove together** below weirdness 60: rock with pop, pop with funk, and the strumming and bass must also fit the drums (no funk drums under rock strumming). Ideas made with the same seeds can sound different from before.
- 2026-10-08 (sound review, part 1: lead, mix space, wrong notes): An exported rock track was measured (no stems needed: the track was still loaded, so it was re-rendered part by part). Problems found: no clear melody, a muddy and harsh mix with a dull top, one random out-of-key chord in every chorus, lead notes grinding against the chords, a stiff funk-drum groove, too much repetition, low loudness, a narrow image. Fixed here: **the lead is the singer** — Guitar 2 melodies sit higher (lead D4–A5, octave melodies G3–A4 plus the octave), driven Guitar 2 tones use a lead voicing (more low cut, a vocal mid push, more sustain, smoother top), rock and hard rock leads use more gain than the rhythm guitar, Guitar 2 is panned near the centre and gets +3 dB (piano as Guitar 2 +4 dB more); in the test chorus the lead went from 3.5 dB under the rhythm guitar to about 1 dB over it. **Mix space** — driven rhythm guitars get a 130 Hz low cut, a cut where they turn harsh in a mix (2.7–2.8 kHz, −2 dB crunch, −4 dB distortion), less treble shelf and a lower top (crunch 8 kHz, distortion 6.5 kHz): the distorted guitar's harsh band is about 5 dB lower; cymbals get +5 dB above 7 kHz (mix top end about 4 dB brighter). **Notes** — the chord generator's "weird chord" no longer picks a fully random chord below weirdness 70: it picks a colour chord bands actually use (major keys: bVII, bVI, bIII, iv, II, III; minor keys: IV, bII, V, V7, bVI, bIII); held melody notes (an 8th or longer, or on the beat) never sit a semitone from a chord tone or a tritone from the root (4.4% of held notes did, now 1.1%). Ideas made with the same seeds can sound different from before. Still to do: groove (accented hi-hats, down-up strumming, no funk drums under rock guitars), variety (quieter verses, less chorus reuse), loudness and stereo width.
- 2026-10-08 (amp switching fix): Changing amp sound at a section (e.g. clean verse → distorted chorus) made a split-second burst. The one electric guitar was re-voiced on a timer: the drive curves changed at once but the volumes 0.1 s later, so the ringing chord went through full distortion at clean-amp volume (peaks +14 dB, about 90 ms before the downbeat), and the first new chord, already scheduled as a single take, was then panned left for its whole length (14 dB lopsided). Now each amp setting is its own guitar, live as well as in exports: nothing changes while notes sound, the old section's last chord rings out on its own amp, and the new section starts on the new one exactly on the beat (measured: no burst, left and right even). Idle amps were also found to be expensive (six idle distorted guitars cost about half a CPU core): the lopsided drive curve had an even number of points, so silence came out as a tiny constant offset that kept every amp processing, and Tone.Filter's always-running setting signals did the same. Drive curves now have an odd number of points, guitars and piano use plain biquad filters (same sound), and the stand-in synths are only made if samples fail. Six idle guitars now cost about 36× less, and audio export got about twice as fast.
